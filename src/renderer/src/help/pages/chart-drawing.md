Drawing tools let you mark up the chart: draw a trendline along a rising low, a line at a price where the stock keeps stopping, or a Fibonacci grid across a recent move. Marking a chart is how traders plan entries, stops and targets, and it is one of the best ways to practise reading a chart.

![A chart with a trendline, rectangle, horizontal lines, a Fibonacci retracement and a marker](drawing-examples.png)

## The drawing menu

The **pen icon** in the chart header opens the drawing menu.

![The drawing tools menu](drawing-menu.png)

| Item | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Cursor (select / edit drawings)** | The normal mode. Pan and zoom the chart, and click drawings to select and edit them |
| **Trendline** | A straight line between two points |
| **Ray** | A line that starts at a point and runs on forever in one direction |
| **Horizontal line** | A line across the whole chart at one price |
| **Fibonacci retracement** | A grid of levels between two points |
| **Rectangle** | A shaded box between two corners |
| **Remove Claude's drawings** | Deletes only the drawings Claude made for you |
| **Remove all drawings** | Deletes every drawing on this symbol |

The pen icon is **highlighted** while a drawing tool is active.

## Drawing a shape

**Trendline, ray, Fibonacci and rectangle** (two points):

1. Choose the tool from the menu. The cursor on the chart turns into a crosshair.
2. Click the **first point** on the chart, then either:
   - **drag** to the second point and let go, or
   - **release** and click the **second point** separately.
3. The shape is placed, and the tool switches back to **Cursor**.

**Horizontal line** (one point):

1. Choose **Horizontal line**.
2. Click at the price you want. The line is placed at once, with its price printed on it.

A few things to know while drawing:

- You can **cancel** a half-finished drawing with **Esc**.
- Each tool draws **one shape and then returns to Cursor**. To draw another, choose the tool again.
- While a tool is active, the chart does **not scroll or zoom**, so your clicks go to the drawing. Press **Esc** to go back.
- Drawings go on the **price chart** only, not on the study panes below it.

## What each tool is for

- **Trendline.** Connect two or more swing lows in an uptrend, or swing highs in a downtrend. Price often respects the line, and a clear break through it can signal a change. A trendline is anchored to its two points, so it keeps its slope as you scroll.
- **Ray.** Like a trendline, but it carries on beyond the second point, which is useful for projecting where a trend line will be.
- **Horizontal line.** Mark **support** (a price where falls tend to stop) or **resistance** (where rises tend to stop), a round number, an earlier high or low, or your planned entry, stop or target.
- **Rectangle.** Highlight a **zone**, such as a consolidation range, a gap, or an area where a stock traded sideways before a breakout.
- **Fibonacci retracement.** After a strong move, traders watch for a pullback to stop near certain proportions of that move. The grid shows each level and its price.

### Reading the Fibonacci grid

The levels drawn are **0, 0.236, 0.382, 0.5, 0.618, 0.786 and 1**, each with a color and its price beside it. A dashed grey line joins your two points.

- Level **1** sits at the point where you **started** and level **0** at the point where you **finished**.
- For a pullback inside an up-move, start at the **swing low** and finish at the **swing high**. The levels then run from the high down toward the low, and the 0.382, 0.5 and 0.618 levels mark possible pullback areas.
- For a down-move, start at the swing high and finish at the swing low.

Fibonacci levels are a **guide to where to look**, not a prediction. Treat them as one clue, combined with support, resistance and the trend.

## Editing a drawing

Choose **Cursor** in the menu, which is also the default after drawing.

| To do this | Do this |
|---|---|
| **Select** a drawing | Click on it. A selected drawing is a little thicker, and trendlines, rays, Fibonacci grids and rectangles show **round handles** at their two points |
| **Move** it | Drag the line or shape itself |
| **Reshape** it | Drag one of the **handles** to a new point |
| **Delete** it | Select it and press **Delete** (or **Backspace**) |
| **Deselect** | Click on empty chart, or press **Esc** |

The pointer changes to a hand when it is over a drawing you can select. A horizontal line has no handles, so drag the line up or down to move it.

## Saving and removing drawings

- Drawings are **saved automatically** for each symbol, so they are still there when you come back.
- They are tied to **time and price**, not to a spot on the screen. They stay in place when you scroll, zoom, or change the **Length** or **Interval**. A drawing made on a daily chart shows up in the right place on an hourly chart too.
- **Remove all drawings** clears every drawing for the current symbol only. It cannot be undone.
- The drawings of other symbols are not affected.

## Drawings from Claude

Claude can draw on your chart while it teaches. Ask something like *Mark the support and resistance levels on this chart*, *Draw a Fibonacci retracement of the last swing* or *Show me where you would put the stop*.

- Claude's drawings can include **labels**, such as *Support* or *Pullback entry*, and Claude can also place **arrow markers** on a candle to point something out.
- They are tagged as Claude's, so **Remove Claude's drawings** clears them without touching your own.
- You can select, move and delete them like any other drawing.

## Colors

Each tool has its own color: blue for trendlines and rays, amber for horizontal lines, purple for Fibonacci (with a different color for each level), light blue for rectangles and yellow for Claude's markers. Drawings made by you use these colors. There is no color picker for drawings yet. Claude can choose a color when it draws, for example green for support and red for resistance.

## Tips

- **Draw before you trade.** Mark the levels first, then decide where an entry, stop and target would go. The order ticket shows these as lines too. See *Order lines on the chart*.
- **Zoom out first.** Levels drawn on a longer time frame (daily or weekly) usually matter more than ones drawn on a 1-minute chart.
- **Keep it simple.** A few clean lines are more useful than a chart covered in drawings.
- **Compare with the studies.** Support and resistance often line up with a moving average or a pivot point. See *Chart settings and studies*.
