Plain-language meanings of the trading terms used in Trading Lab. Where a term works in a particular way in the app, the entry says so and points to the page that explains it.

## A

**Account type** — Whether a paper account is a **margin** account (2:1 buying power, short selling allowed) or a **cash** account (buying power is your cash, no short selling). See *Account*.

**Action** — On the order ticket, whether you are **Buy**, **Sell**, **Sell short** or **Buy to cover**. See *The order ticket*.

**Active account** — The paper account the app is working on right now. New orders go to it, and Active Trades, the calculator and Claude use it. It is named in the status bar. See *Account*.

**ADX (Average Directional Index)** — A study that measures how **strong** a trend is, whichever way it points. A reading above 25 is usually called a strong trend. See *Chart settings and studies*.

**Aftermarket** — Trading outside regular hours, before the open and after the close. Quote details shows the latest aftermarket trade and quote. See *Quote details*.

**Allocation** — How your money is split between holdings, as percentages. In the *Portfolio* screen each holding has a **target** percentage. See *Portfolio*.

**Ask** — The lowest price a seller is currently willing to accept. You pay the ask when you buy at the market. Compare **bid**.

**ATR (Average True Range)** — The average size of a candle's high-to-low range over a set number of candles (14 by default). It measures how much a stock typically moves, and it is used to place stops and to size positions. See *Position calculator*.

**Average price** — What you paid per share on average, across all your buys of a position. Shown as **Avg price** in Active trades.

## B

**Backup** — A single file holding your accounts, orders, journal, watchlists and settings, made with **File → Back Up Data…**. It does not include API keys. See *Data, cache and backups*.

**Bar** — One period of price on a chart: a candle with its open, high, low and close. Also the name of a chart type (OHLC bars).

**Bear market, bearish** — A falling market, or an expectation that prices will fall.

**Beta** — How much a stock tends to move compared with the market. About 1 moves with the market, above 1 moves more, and below 1 moves less. See *Quote details*.

**Bid** — The highest price a buyer is currently offering. You receive the bid when you sell at the market. The gap between bid and ask is the **spread**.

**Bollinger Bands** — A study that draws a moving average with a band above and below it, set a number of standard deviations away. The bands widen when the price is more volatile.

**Bracket order** — An entry order placed together with a **profit target** and a **stop**. When the entry fills, the other two start, and when one of them fills, the other is cancelled. See *Order types, brackets and OCO*.

**Breakout** — A move of price above a resistance level, or below a support level, often with high volume. See *Trading strategies library*.

**Bull market, bullish** — A rising market, or an expectation that prices will rise.

**Buying power** — How much you can still use to open new positions. In a margin paper account it is twice your equity minus the market value of what you hold; in a cash account it is your cash. Orders waiting to fill are set aside. See *Account*.

## C

**Call** — An option that gives the right to **buy** a stock at a set price. It gains value when the stock rises. See *Options*.

**Candlestick** — A way of drawing one period of price: a body between the open and close, and thin lines (wicks) up to the high and down to the low. See *The chart screen*.

**Close** — The last price of a period. The **previous close** is the last price of the prior session.

**Consensus** — The overall view of a group of analysts, shown as a rating (such as Buy) and an average price target. See *Analyst reports*.

**Cost basis** — What you paid for a holding, including fees. The *Portfolio* screen calls it **Total paid**, and gain or loss is the value now minus the cost basis.

**Cover** — To buy back shares you sold short, closing the short position (**Buy to cover**).

**Crosshair** — The lines that follow your mouse over the chart and show the exact price and time. It can be **Free** or **Magnet** (snapping to the nearest candle's open, high, low or close).

## D

**Day order (DAY)** — An order that expires at the close of today's session if it has not filled. Compare **GTC**.

**Delta** — For an option, how much its price moves for a $1 move in the stock. It is also a rough chance of finishing in the money. See *Options*.

**Dividend** — A payment some companies make to shareholders. The **dividend yield** is the yearly dividend as a percent of the price.

**Drawdown** — A fall from a high point in the value of an account or a position.

**Drift** — How far a holding's actual share of your portfolio has moved from its target, in percentage points. Winners drift above target, losers below. See *Holdings and value*.

## E

**EMA (Exponential Moving Average)** — A moving average that gives more weight to recent prices, so it reacts faster than a simple average.

**Entry** — The price at which a trade is opened.

**EPS (Earnings per share)** — A company's profit divided by its number of shares. **TTM** EPS covers the last twelve months. See *Fundamentals*.

**Equity** — Your account's total value: cash plus the current value of your positions. Also called **net liquidation value**.

**ETF (Exchange-traded fund)** — A fund that trades on an exchange like a stock, usually tracking an index or a theme.

**Expected move** — The range the options market implies for a stock by an expiration date. See *Options*.

**Expiration** — The date an option contract ends.

## F

**Fibonacci retracement** — A set of horizontal levels (0, 0.236, 0.382, 0.5, 0.618, 0.786 and 1) drawn between two points of a move. Traders watch them as possible places for a pullback to stop. See *Drawing tools*.

**Fill** — The execution of an order, at a particular price. See *How orders are filled*.

**FMP plan** — The subscription level of your market-data account. It decides which data you can get, such as which chart intervals and screens. Trading Lab checks it and hides what it does not include. See *API keys*.

**Free cash flow** — Cash left after a company pays to run and maintain its business. See *Fundamentals*.

**Fundamentals** — Information about a company's business: sales, profit, debt, valuation. Compare **technical analysis**, which studies the price chart.

## G

**Gamma** — For an option, how fast its delta changes as the stock moves.

**Gap** — A jump between one candle's close and the next one's open, with no trading in between. Stops and limit orders may fill at the new price, not the one you set. See *How orders are filled*.

**GTC (Good till cancelled)** — An order that stays active until it fills or you cancel it. The target and stop of a bracket are always GTC.

## H

**Heikin Ashi** — A chart type that averages candles to smooth the trend. Its prices are calculated, not real trades.

**High, low** — The highest and lowest prices of a period.

**Historical volatility** — How much a stock actually moved over a past period. Compare **implied volatility**.

## I

**Implied volatility (IV)** — The amount of movement that option prices imply for the future, as a yearly percent. See *Options*.

**In the money (ITM)** — A call whose strike is below the stock price, or a put whose strike is above it.

**Interval** — The time each candle covers, such as 5 minutes or a day. See *Length and interval*.

## J

**Journal** — Your record of why you take trades and what you learn. See *Trading journal*.

## K

**Keltner Channels** — A study that draws an EMA with bands set a multiple of ATR above and below it.

## L

**Length** — How much history the chart shows. See *Length and interval*.

**Limit order** — An order to buy at a set price or lower, or to sell at a set price or higher. It guarantees the price but not that it fills.

**Liquidity** — How easily a stock can be bought and sold without moving its price. Stocks with high volume are more liquid.

**Long** — Owning shares, expecting the price to rise.

## M

**MACD (Moving Average Convergence Divergence)** — A study built from two EMAs, with a signal line and a histogram, used to read momentum.

**Margin** — Borrowed buying power. A margin paper account allows 2:1; a cash account has none. See *Account*.

**Market cap** — A company's total value: share price times the number of shares. *Large-cap* means a big company and *small-cap* a smaller one.

**Market order** — An order to buy or sell immediately at the best available price. It guarantees the trade but not the price.

**Momentum** — The speed and strength of a price move. Studies such as RSI and MACD measure it.

**Moving average** — The average price over a set number of candles. It smooths the price to show the trend. See *Chart settings and studies*.

**Mutual fund** — A pooled fund you buy from the fund company rather than on an exchange. It is priced once a day after the close, at its net asset value, and can be bought in fractions of a share. See *Portfolio*.

## N

**NAV (Net asset value)** — The value of one share of a mutual fund (or ETF's holdings): total assets minus liabilities, divided by the number of shares. Mutual funds trade at the NAV calculated after the close.

**Net P/L** — Total profit or loss after everything, as in the journal's summary.

## O

**OCO (One cancels the other)** — Two orders linked so that when one fills, the other is cancelled. See *Order types, brackets and OCO*.

**Open** — The first price of a period.

**Open interest (OI)** — The number of option contracts currently open.

**Open P/L** — Profit or loss on positions you still hold. Also called unrealized P/L.

**Order ticket** — The form for setting up an order. See *The order ticket*.

**Out of the money (OTM)** — A call whose strike is above the stock price, or a put whose strike is below it.

**Overbought, oversold** — Descriptions of a price that has risen or fallen a long way quickly. RSI above 70 is often called overbought and below 30 oversold. Neither is a signal on its own.

## P

**P/B (Price to book)** — The share price compared with a company's net assets.

**P/E (Price to earnings)** — The share price divided by earnings per share. How many dollars investors pay for each dollar of yearly profit.

**P/S (Price to sales)** — Market cap divided by revenue.

**Paper trading** — Trading with simulated money to practise. You can keep several paper accounts, each mirroring a real one. See *How paper trading works* and *Account*.

**PEG** — The P/E divided by expected earnings growth.

**Pivot points** — Levels worked out from a previous period's high, low and close, used as possible support and resistance.

**Position** — Shares you currently hold or are short.

**Position sizing** — Deciding how many shares to trade from the amount you are willing to risk. See *Position calculator*.

**Pre-market** — Trading before the regular open.

**Price target** — An analyst's estimate of where a stock will trade in about a year.

**Profit target** — The price at which you plan to take your gain.

**Pullback** — A temporary dip within an uptrend. See *Trading strategies library*.

**Put** — An option that gives the right to **sell** a stock at a set price. It gains value when the stock falls.

**Put/call ratio** — Put volume or open interest divided by call volume or open interest. A rough gauge of the crowd's mood.

## R

**R (R multiple)** — A result measured in units of your planned risk. If you risked $100 and made $200, you made **+2R**. See *Trading journal*.

**Rate of Change (ROC)** — A study showing the percent change in price over a set number of candles.

**Realized P/L** — Profit or loss locked in by closed trades.

**Rebalancing** — Bringing a portfolio back to its target mix, by adding money to what has fallen behind or selling some of what has grown. The *Portfolio* screen shows the drift and, in *Total size* mode, how to top up. It never sells for you.

**Relative volume** — Today's volume divided by the stock's average volume. It finds stocks that are unusually busy. See *Stock screener*.

**Resistance** — A price level where rises have repeatedly stopped.

**Restore** — Replacing your current data with a backup, using **File → Restore From Backup…**. A safety copy of the current data is saved first. See *Data, cache and backups*.

**Reward-to-risk** — The size of the potential gain compared with the size of the potential loss. A ratio of 2:1 means you aim to make twice what you risk.

**Risk per trade** — The amount you will lose if the stop is hit, usually set as a percent of the account.

**ROE, ROA** — Return on equity and return on assets: profit as a percent of shareholders' money, and of everything a company owns.

**RSI (Relative Strength Index)** — A momentum study from 0 to 100.

## S

**Screener** — A tool that filters thousands of stocks down to a list that fits your rules. See *Stock screener*.

**Sector, industry** — Groups of similar companies. Sectors are the broad groups and industries are the narrower ones. See *Market performance*.

**Short** — Selling shares you do not own, expecting the price to fall, and buying them back later (**cover**).

**SMA (Simple Moving Average)** — The plain average of the last N closing prices.

**Slippage** — The difference between the price you expected and the price you got. The paper account does not model it. See *How paper trading works*.

**Spread** — The gap between the bid and the ask.

**Stop (stop order)** — An order that triggers when the price reaches a level, then trades at the market. A **stop loss** is a stop placed to limit a loss.

**Stop-limit** — A stop that, once triggered, becomes a limit order.

**Strike** — The set price in an option contract.

**Supertrend** — A trend study that draws one line that follows the price and changes color when the trend turns.

**Support** — A price level where falls have repeatedly stopped.

**Swing high, swing low** — A local peak or trough in price, used to place stops and draw trendlines.

## T

**Take profits** — Selling part of a holding that has gone up, to lock in some of the gain. The *Sell* tab of the Portfolio screen plans it as a percentage. See *Selling a share of the portfolio*.

**Technical analysis** — Studying price charts and indicators to judge what may happen next. Compare **fundamentals**.

**Theta** — For an option, how much value it loses each day from the passage of time.

**Time in force** — How long an order stays active: **DAY** or **GTC**.

**Trailing stop** — A stop that follows the price at a fixed distance and never moves against you.

**Trend** — The general direction of price. An uptrend has higher highs and higher lows.

**Trim** — Selling a little of a holding that has grown beyond its target, rather than all of it. *Overweight first* on the Sell tab does this.

**TTM (Trailing twelve months)** — The most recent four quarters added together.

## U

**Update** — A newer release of Trading Lab on GitHub. **About → Check for updates** tells you if one exists and links to the download. See *Updates and versions*.

## V

**Vega** — For an option, how much its price changes for a 1-point change in implied volatility.

**VIX** — An index of expected stock market volatility, shown in the index ticker.

**Volatility** — How much and how fast a price moves.

**Volume** — The number of shares (or contracts) traded in a period.

**VWAP (Volume-weighted average price)** — The average price weighted by volume. In Trading Lab it is counted from the first candle on the chart.

## W

**Watchlist** — A saved list of symbols you follow. See *Watchlists*.

**Win rate** — The percent of trades that make money. The **break-even win rate** is the win rate you need at a given reward-to-risk to avoid losing money.

**Working order** — An order that has been placed but has not yet filled.

## Y

**Yield** — See **dividend**.
