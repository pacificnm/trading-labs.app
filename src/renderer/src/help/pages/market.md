**Market Performance** shows how the whole market is doing today, broken down by **sector** and **industry**, with the day's **biggest gainers, losers and most active stocks**. Open it with the **bar chart** icon on the ribbon. It helps you see where money is flowing before you pick individual stocks.

![Sector performance and sector valuation](market-sectors.png)

## The controls

Along the top of the screen:

- **Exchange**: choose **NASDAQ**, **NYSE** or **AMEX**. The sector and industry figures cover stocks listed on that exchange.
- **Day**: a date picker. It starts on the **latest trading day**. Pick an earlier date to look back, and a **Latest** button appears to go back to the newest day. If you choose a weekend or a holiday, or there is nothing yet for today (before the open), the screen falls back to the most recent day that has data.
- **Data as of** shows when the information was fetched. The screen refreshes itself about **every minute**, and **Refresh** fetches the latest right away.

A line below the controls sums it up, for example *7 of 11 sectors are up on NASDAQ-listed stocks*. It reminds you that sector and industry figures are the **average change across the listed stocks** in each group (which is not the same as an index) and that the movers lists are **live**.

## Sector performance

A grid of tiles, one for each of the 11 market sectors, such as Technology, Healthcare, Energy and Utilities.

- Each tile shows the sector name and its **average percent change** for the day.
- **Green** tiles are up and **red** tiles are down. The deeper the color, the bigger the move.
- The tiles are ordered **best performer first**.
- **Hover** over a tile to see its P/E ratio too.

Look for the pattern, not just the leaders. When growth sectors such as Technology rise while defensive sectors such as Utilities and Consumer Defensive fall, investors are generally feeling confident (*risk on*). When the defensive sectors lead and growth sectors fall, investors are generally cautious (*risk off*). Money moving from one group to another is called **rotation**.

## Sector valuation

Bars showing the **price-to-earnings ratio (P/E)** of each sector, highest first, with the number at the end of each bar.

- A **P/E** says how many dollars investors pay for each dollar of yearly profit. See *Fundamentals*.
- An **amber marker** on every bar shows the **average of all the sectors**, so you can see which sectors are priced above or below the pack.
- The card heading states the average value.

A sector with a high P/E, like technology, is one where investors expect strong growth. A low P/E, like energy or real estate, is usually a slower or more cyclical area. The numbers are **comparisons, not verdicts**, because sectors differ by nature. A sector that is expensive compared with its own history is more worth noticing than one that is expensive compared with others.

## Industries

A table of the industries inside the sectors (dozens of them, such as *Semiconductors*, *Biotechnology*, *Banks - Regional* and *Gold*).

![The industries table](market-industries.png)

| Column | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Industry** | The name of the group |
| **Change** | The average percent change today, with a bar that grows to the **right for gains** and to the **left for losses** |
| **P/E** | The average P/E of the stocks in that industry |

- **Click a column heading** to sort by it. Click again to reverse the order. The arrow beside the heading shows the direction. It starts sorted by **Change**, best first.
- Type in **Filter industries…** to narrow the list. The heading shows how many industries match, for example *4 of 20*.
- The table scrolls inside its card.

Industries tell you more than sectors do. A strong sector can hide one industry that is falling hard, and a weak sector can have one that is rising. Use this table to find the industry behind a sector move, then look for the stocks in it.

## Movers

![The biggest gainers, losers and most active lists](market-movers.png)

Three lists of up to 12 stocks each:

- **Biggest gainers**: the largest percentage rises today.
- **Biggest losers**: the largest percentage falls.
- **Most active**: the stocks with the most shares traded.

Each row shows the **symbol** and company name, the **price** and the **percent change**. Hover a row for the name and exchange, and **click a row** to open that stock's chart.

Unlike the sector tables, the movers lists are **live** and are not tied to the **Day** you chose.

### Filters

A row of filters above the lists keeps junk out:

- **Min price**: **Any**, **$1+**, **$5+**, **$10+** or **$20+**. It starts at **$5+**.
- **Exchange**: **All**, **NASDAQ**, **NYSE** or **AMEX**.
- **Hide funds & ETFs**: on by default. It removes mutual funds and anything named like an ETF, fund or trust.

If the filters hide every stock, the card says *Nothing matches your filters*.

> **A warning about movers.** Raw lists of the biggest gainers and losers are dominated by **very cheap, thinly traded stocks** that jump or crash on tiny volume. They are hard to trade, their prices can be unreliable, and they are an easy place to lose money. The default filters hide most of them. A large move on high volume in a stock you can trade matters far more than a huge move in a penny stock.

## Putting it to use

- **Start broad.** Which sectors lead and lag today? Is it rotation, or is everything moving together?
- **Narrow down.** Look at the industries inside the leading sector, then at the movers.
- **Check why.** Click a mover, then open its news on the chart header. See *Symbol news*. A move with a clear reason is more reliable than one without.
- **Look at the setup.** Open the chart and decide whether the move fits a plan before you trade it. See *The chart screen*.
- **Compare valuation.** A sector that has risen strongly while its P/E is already the highest on the list may be stretched.

## When there is no data

- **Without an FMP key**, the screen asks you to add one. Click the link to go to Settings. See *First-time setup*.
- **An error message with Retry** appears if the data could not be loaded.
- *No sector data for this day and exchange* if the exchange has nothing for the chosen day. Some data may need a higher FMP plan, and **Settings → Test connection** shows what your plan includes.
