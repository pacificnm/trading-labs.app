A **watchlist** is a list of stocks you want to keep an eye on. It saves you from searching for the same symbols again and again, and shows their latest prices side by side. You can keep **several lists** for different purposes, such as *Core holdings*, *Earnings this week* or *Ideas to research*. Open the Watchlists screen with the **checklist** icon on the ribbon.

![The Watchlists screen](watchlists-screen.png)

> Looking to plan percentages and shares, or to track what you own? That is the **Portfolio** screen. See *Portfolio*.

## Your lists

The tabs along the top are your lists. Each shows its **name** and how many symbols it has. The first time you run the app you have one list called **Watchlist** holding AAPL, MSFT, NVDA, TSLA and SPY, which you can change freely.

| To do this | Do this |
|---|---|
| **Switch list** | Click its tab |
| **Create a list** | Click the **+** at the end of the tabs, type a name and click **Create** |
| **Rename a list** | Double-click its tab, or click the **pencil** icon at the right, then type the new name |
| **Delete a list** | Click the **bin** icon at the right and confirm |

![The New watchlist box](watchlists-new.png)

- List names can be up to **40 characters** and must be **different** from each other.
- You always need **at least one list**, so the delete button is greyed out when only one is left.
- Deleting a list removes the list and its symbols from the app. It does **not** touch positions, orders or your journal.

## The symbols

Under the tabs, the table shows the selected list.

| Column | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Symbol** | The ticker. The row for the symbol you are currently viewing on the chart is highlighted |
| **Last** | The latest price |
| **Change** | The change since the previous close, in dollars, green or red |
| **%** | The same change as a percent |
| **X** | Removes the symbol from this list |

- **Click a row** to open that symbol on the chart.
- Prices update about **every 30 seconds** while the screen is open. Without an FMP key, or if a price cannot be loaded, the figures show a dash. See *First-time setup*.
- An empty list says so, and reminds you how to add the first symbol.

## Adding symbols

### On this screen

Type a ticker in the box above the table and press **Enter** or click **Add**. The box turns what you type into capitals, and its hint names the list you are adding to, for example *Add a symbol to Watchlist (e.g. AMZN) and press Enter*.

- A ticker can use letters, numbers and the characters `.` `^` `=` and `-`, up to 15 characters long.
- If the symbol is already there, a message says so and nothing changes.
- An invalid ticker gets a message explaining that it is not a valid symbol.

### From the chart

The **star** at the left of the chart header adds the symbol you are viewing. It opens a menu of all your lists, so you can tick several lists at once, or type a name there to create a new list that already contains the symbol. See *Finding symbols*.

### From the screener

On the **Stock screener**, the **+** at the end of a row adds that stock to your active list, and **Save top 50 as watchlist** turns a whole result list into a new watchlist. See *Stock screener*.

### From Claude

Ask Claude to track stocks for you, for example *Add NVDA and AMD to my Earnings watch list*. Claude can read your lists, add symbols (and creates a list if it does not exist yet) and remove them. See *What Claude can do*.

## Where your lists show up

Watchlists are used across the app.

- **The chart's top bar** has a **watchlist dropdown** with the active list, a price and percent change for each symbol, and a quick way to jump to the chart. See *Finding symbols*.
- **Market news** has a **Watchlist** tab with the news for every symbol on a list. See *Market news*.
- **Claude** can see your lists and use them when you ask about *my watchlist*.

The list you have selected on this screen becomes the **active list**, and is the one the screener's **+** button, the top bar dropdown and Claude use by default.

## Using watchlists well

- **Keep lists short and purposeful.** Ten stocks you follow closely beat a list of a hundred you never look at.
- **Group by reason.** One list for stocks you hold, one for stocks waiting for a setup, one for research. The name tells you why a stock is there.
- **Decide what triggers action.** For each stock, write down the price or event that would make you act, in the journal if you like. See *Trading journal*.
- **Check them at the same time each day.** A routine builds the feel for how stocks normally behave.
- **Prune regularly.** Remove stocks that no longer interest you.
